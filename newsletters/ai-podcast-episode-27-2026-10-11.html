PODCAST #27

October 11, 2026

Personal AI agents arrived in force this month. Meta's Muse and OpenAI's dots now work on a person's behalf, Google has announced its own agent, and Muse had an estimated 2.8 million downloads in its first two weeks. That puts companies on the receiving end in a new position: Amazon blocked Muse from its site, while Meta, Sierra and dozens of partners drafted a protocol for agents and businesses to work together. Security concerns have followed the launches, and small models like Claude Haiku 5.5 are making AI cheaper to run. A JAMA study of AI scribes found the biggest savings among the heaviest users, and Gallup's numbers show only a quarter of employees say their organization has shared a clear AI plan. So who is ready, and who gets left on the sidelines?

Personal Agents Arrive

Within a month, Meta, OpenAI and Google each launched or announced an agent that acts on a person's behalf. Sensor Tower estimated 2.8 million Muse downloads in its first two weeks, and The Information reported more than a million people prompt it daily. Read more →

What Personal Agents Mean for Business

Amazon blocked Muse and asked Meta to remove it, and a Ninth Circuit ruling left the legal question partly open. Meta and Sierra's Personal Agent Protocol, with 35 more design partners added October 9, lets companies route and manage agents. Read more →

Security Concerns Follow Muse

404 Media reported a pre-launch flaw that could let an ordinary user escape their agent's virtual machine and reach internal Meta databases. Apple announced new Full Disk Access controls, and TIME reported that Muse builds profiles of the people users mention. Read more →

Small Models, Lower Prices

Claude Haiku 5.5 is priced 90 percent lower than Haiku 4.5 for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, and OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 with Intelligent UI in ChatGPT, with Free and Go users getting GPT-6 Luna. Read more →

AI Scribes: Modest Gains, Uneven Use

A JAMA study of five health systems found clinicians using AI scribes spent 16 minutes less on documentation per eight scheduled patient hours, and those using them in at least half their visits saved 27.3 minutes. Read more →

Who Is Being Left Behind

Gallup found 15 percent of US employees use AI daily and 25 percent say their organization has shared a clear integration plan. Microsoft found 28.8 percent usage in the Global North against 16.2 percent in the Global South. Read more →
