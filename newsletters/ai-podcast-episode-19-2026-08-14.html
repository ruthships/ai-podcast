This week wasn’t about a single model or product — it was about who actually controls artificial intelligence. OpenAI lost two more senior executives within 48 hours, adding to a pattern of turnover just as the company preps for a possible IPO. OpenAI’s Executive Exodus Deepens Amid IPO Prep and a Safety Scandal

OpenAI lost two senior executives within 48 hours in August — CFO-turned-COO Brad Lightcap and chief revenue officer Denise Dresser, replaced by former Wiz president Dali Rajic. The departures add to a pattern of turnover this year, alongside a $7 billion employee share buyback and confidential IPO paperwork.

The Open-Weight vs. Closed-Weight Fight Boils Over

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang published an open letter urging open-weight AI, quickly signed by fifty companies including Microsoft and Meta. OpenAI and Google both signed within days; Anthropic refused. Export controls once let regulators disable Anthropic’s models for foreign users overnight — proof, open-weight advocates say, that closed models can be switched off.

Nvidia and AMD Turn AI Infrastructure Into a $500 Billion Asset Class

Nvidia signed agreements with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, and other major investors to build a $500 billion financing platform treating AI chips and data centers like a new investable asset class. AMD, meanwhile, posted 50% revenue growth and moved to raise up to $5 billion in bonds for its own buildout.

Cohere Goes Global — Canada’s AI Champion Expands Into Asia-Pacific

Canadian AI company Cohere is opening a standalone South Korea subsidiary and a new Japan office, doubling its Asia-Pacific workforce after its regional customer base grew fivefold in a year. The expansion includes an exclusive partnership with South Korean IT firm LG CNS, built on enterprise and sovereign-AI customers.

Apple v. OpenAI Trade Secrets Fight Escalates

Apple is seeking a preliminary injunction against OpenAI and Jony Ive’s hardware startup io, alleging their hardware was built on stolen Apple trade secrets, with a hearing set for October 1st. OpenAI has moved to dismiss the suit, calling it “rotten to its core,” with its formal response due August 17th.
