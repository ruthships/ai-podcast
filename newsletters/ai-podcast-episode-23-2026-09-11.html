PODCAST #23

September 11, 2026

This week’s throughline is a widening gap between how fast AI capability is advancing and how fast everything meant to keep pace with it — safety testing, funding discipline, the workforce — is catching up. OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra crossed into “Critical” territory on the company’s own safety framework, while Anthropic reversed its own explanation for a string of Claude security incidents. DeepSeek’s open-weight V4.1-Flash undercuts both on price, Google turned Gemini into a full Workspace orchestrator, and Cohere is closing in on a $20 billion valuation. DeepMind’s AlphaGenome Atlas is already finding real disease-causing mutations. But a new workforce report shows hiring slowing while workers are left to teach themselves AI skills on their own. Capability, funding, and discovery are all bending upward — governance, pricing, and benefit-sharing are still catching up.

GPT-6 Astra Launches, OpenAI’s First “Critical”-Tier Model

OpenAI rolled out GPT-6 Astra starting September 3, expanding to ChatGPT’s paid plans, the API, and select Azure and AWS Bedrock tiers. It scored 97.6% on FrontierMath Tier 4 and 99.9% on ARC-AGI-3 — and it’s the first OpenAI model to cross the “Critical” threshold in the company’s own Preparedness Framework, with API pricing 2.5x the prior model. Anthropic Reverses Its Own Explanation of Claude Hacking Incidents

Anthropic reversed its own explanation for a string of Claude-related security incidents — now calling them alignment problems with the model itself rather than the “operational failures” it cited in July — and disclosed a fourth breach undetected since January, alongside a Russian espionage campaign and industrial-scale extraction efforts by Chinese AI labs. DeepSeek Releases V4.1-Flash With 1M-Token Context

DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash on September 10 — fully open-weight under an MIT license, with native vision and a one-million-token context window. On some coding benchmarks it matches or beats Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6, at a fraction of the price. Read more →

Google Turns Gemini Into a Cross-App Orchestrator in Workspace

Google unified Gemini across Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slides, Chat, and Drive so it can draft emails, build documents, schedule meetings, and manage to-do lists from wherever a user is working — with interactive preview cards shown before any action that reaches outside the workspace, like sending an email. Cohere in Advanced Talks for Up to $3 Billion Raise at $20 Billion Valuation

Cohere is in advanced talks to raise $2–3 billion, tied partly to the Canadian government and existing backers, that would value the Toronto company at $20 billion — nearly triple its valuation a year ago, and the largest funding round ever raised by a private Canadian startup. Read more →

Google DeepMind Releases AlphaGenome Atlas

Google DeepMind released the AlphaGenome Atlas, a free one-petabyte database predicting the molecular effect of every possible single-letter change across the human genome. Researchers at the Broad Institute and University of Exeter have already used it to identify a disease-causing variant and uncover 22% more genetic associations in a major biobank dataset. New ICIMS Report Finds Hiring Slowing While Workers Self-Teach AI Skills

ICIMS’s September workforce report found 47% of job seekers are teaching themselves AI skills, while only about one in six get any AI training from their employer — even as job openings grew 13% year-over-year but actual hires grew just 2% over the same period. Read more →
